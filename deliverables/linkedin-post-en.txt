The invoice says $4,271.98. The extracted fields say $42,719.80. Every arithmetic check passes.

I deliberately multiplied all four amounts by ten to test a gap in document automation: correct arithmetic can still disagree with the page.

My open Document Verification Lab combines MiniCPM-OCR with OpenBMB's 2B OCR Jev. The verifier compares the original image, candidate text and a rendered copy. A classification head converts that comparison into a consistency score for review routing.

The video follows one dollar invoice from upload to human correction, then shows how corrected fields could feed PO matching and approval.

The results:
• On 80 held-out synthetic invoices, rules caught all 3 natural errors. The verifier added 13 reviews and caught no extra errors.
• In a separate stress test, it flagged all 24 deliberately altered candidates that passed the rules.

My takeaway: measure extra errors caught alongside extra review work. That's what determines whether another model earns its place.

Released models, no fine-tuning. Cases, scores and code are open.
