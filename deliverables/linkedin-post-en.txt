The numbers added up. The invoice was still wrong.

I built a small Document Verification Lab to explore a question behind document automation: how much does a visual verifier add after the checks we already have?

The setup uses OpenBMB's MiniCPM-OCR to read invoice regions and its 2B OCR Jev model to compare the source pixels, candidate text and a rendered copy of that text. The verifier returns a consistency score, which can help route questionable results to review.

I ran it on 120 generated invoices: 40 for choosing a review threshold and 80 held out.

The result that mattered most: simple rules caught all 3 natural errors in the test set. Adding the verifier sent 13 correct results to review and caught no additional errors.

Then I tested a different failure mode separately: change every currency label, or multiply every amount by ten. The arithmetic still works. Rules missed all 24 of these deliberately altered candidates; the visual verifier flagged all 24.

My takeaway: evaluate a new model against what your workflow already knows. Count the extra errors it catches and the extra reviews it creates. Keep designed stress tests separate from naturally occurring mistakes.

This feels relevant to supplier-invoice intake and other document workflows where an extracted value needs traceable evidence. Reading the page correctly is one check; matching a purchase order is another.

The lab lets you inspect every case and move the threshold yourself. Inputs, raw scores, exact model revisions and evaluation code are public. This uses released models without fine-tuning.
